Produce an artifact that explains the topic we've been discussing.

Be precise and concise. Leave no stones unturned, but keep it easy to
understand and practical: a 5-10 minute read, not a textbook.

Don't over-index on the specific problem I'm working on. Use my case only
when a concrete example makes a concept land.

Lean on structure and visuals where they make things clearer: comparison
tables, a decision tree, a labelled diagram. A picture of the mechanism
beats a paragraph describing it.

Cite primary, high-trust sources for any non-obvious claim and link them.
Don't rely on parametric knowledge alone.

This should stand on its own as a polished, complete reference: precise,
well-structured, and visually clear. It doesn't need a hook or narrative
voice, it needs to be correct and easy to navigate.

Calibrate vocabulary, analogies, and depth to the audience below. Don't
write generic and label it after.

Audience: $ARGUMENTS — if empty, default to someone technical but new to
this specific topic.

Before you write, load the `artifact-design` skill to calibrate the design
effort, then ask any clarifying questions about scope or depth.
